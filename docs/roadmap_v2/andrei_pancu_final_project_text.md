# Andrei Pancu — Final Project Text / Practice Description

This body of work centers on chemically altered steel plates and archival prints generated from them. Repeated stamped pattern introduces a disciplined visual order, while oxidation, patina, sealing, and other material changes keep the surface active and unstable.[file:1][file:5]

Time is a fundamental dimension of the practice because the plates continue to change. Rather than treating the artwork as a fixed image, the work stages a relation between process and arrest: the plate remains in motion, while photography and print preserve a selected threshold that the original surface cannot permanently hold.[file:4][file:5]

The stamped motifs operate as semiotic fragments. They are not fixed symbols with singular meanings, but partial signs or coded visual units that prompt viewers to assemble significance through repetition, interruption, and visual association.[file:1][file:4][file:5]

The project therefore brings together order and instability as simultaneous conditions. Structure is introduced through repeated marking, but chemical transformation prevents the image from fully settling, making uncertainty part of the work’s logic rather than a departure from it.[file:1][file:4]

Secondary extensions include time-lapse video that documents patina progression and small free-standing magnetic metal constructions assembled from plates or fragments. These remain supporting forms that extend the language of the work without displacing its core focus on plates and prints.[file:1][file:5]

Future expansion may include larger plates and larger original works, but the current public presentation should remain anchored in the coherence of the existing body: chemically altered plates as source objects and archival prints as the primary exhibition format.[file:1]
