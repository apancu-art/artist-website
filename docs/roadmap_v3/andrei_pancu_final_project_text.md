# Andrei Pancu — Final Project Text

This practice develops a body of work based on chemically altered steel plates and archival prints made from them. Repeated stamped pattern creates a disciplined visual order, while oxidation, patina, sealing, and other material shifts keep the surface active and unstable.[file:1][file:5]

Time is not a side theme in the work. The plates continue to change, so the piece is not only what is seen in a given moment but also what remains in motion after that moment has passed.[file:4][file:5]

Photography and archival print preserve transient states the plate itself cannot keep. In that sense, the print is not a reproduction in the ordinary sense; it is a way of holding a stage of transformation long enough for it to be encountered as an image.[file:4][file:5]

The stamped motifs function as semiotic fragments. They are partial signs rather than fixed symbols, and they invite viewers to construct meaning through repetition, interruption, and association.[file:1][file:4][file:5]

The project’s secondary extensions include time-lapse video documenting patina progression and small free-standing magnetic metal constructions. These remain related but subordinate forms, useful when they clarify the work’s temporal logic or extend the language into object and installation form.[file:1][file:5]

If the practice expands further, larger plate works may become possible later. For now, the public presentation should remain anchored in the clearest and strongest hierarchy: plates and archival prints first, with the other formats positioned as extensions.
