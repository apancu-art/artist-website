Andrei Pancu works with steel plates as changing surfaces rather than fixed supports. He brushes, stamps, and chemically alters the metal, then photographs selected stages and translates them into prints, preserving states that the plate itself cannot hold. Repeated motifs function in his work as semiotic fragments: partial signs, coded marks, and visual cues that invite viewers to complete the narrative internally. Time is central to the practice. Where most visual art is encountered as a stable object, Pancu’s plates continue to shift, making duration, decay, and arrested transformation part of the image itself. Recent extensions of the work include time-lapse recordings of patina development and small free-standing magnetic constructions built from metal plates and fragments.

The resulting body of work moves between image, object, and process. It treats order and instability as coexisting conditions, and uses print not simply to reproduce the plate but to hold a moment of transition visible long enough to be read.
