# Andrei Pancu Master Launch Packet v3

## Identity and Positioning

**Public name:** Andrei Pancu.

**Primary biographical frame:** Montreal.

**Operational base:** Ottawa.

**Public tone:** Hybrid — formal for grants and institutional applications, slightly more lyrical for exhibitions and wall text.

Andrei Pancu is a contemporary artist whose work begins with chemically altered steel plates and extends into photography, print, time-based documentation, and small sculptural arrangements. The practice centers on surfaces that remain active, so image, object, and process are never entirely separable.

## Revised Bio

Andrei Pancu is a contemporary artist born in Bucharest, Romania, and based in Montreal and Ottawa. His practice uses chemically altered steel plates, stamped pattern, photography, and print to explore surfaces that change over time. In his work, repeating motifs function as semiotic fragments: signs that invite viewers to construct meaning while material change keeps the image unsettled. Pancu also develops related time-based and sculptural studies, including time-lapse documentation of patina progression and small magnetic plate constructions. He studied Fine Arts at the Université de Montréal.

## Revised Artist Statement

Andrei Pancu works with steel plates as changing surfaces rather than fixed supports. He brushes, stamps, and chemically alters the metal, then photographs selected stages and translates them into prints, preserving states that the plate itself cannot hold. Repeated motifs function in his work as semiotic fragments: partial signs, coded marks, and visual cues that invite viewers to complete the narrative internally. Time is central to the practice. Where most visual art is encountered as a stable object, Pancu’s plates continue to shift, making duration, decay, and arrested transformation part of the image itself. Recent extensions of the work include time-lapse recordings of patina development and small free-standing magnetic constructions built from metal plates and fragments.

The resulting body of work moves between image, object, and process. It treats order and instability as coexisting conditions, and uses print not simply to reproduce the plate but to hold a moment of transition visible long enough to be read.

## Revised Project Text

This project develops a body of prints, plates, and related works generated from chemically altered steel surfaces. Repeated stamped patterns introduce a structured visual system, but the metal remains active, so oxidation, sealing, and time continue to alter the image. Photography preserves selected thresholds in that process, while archival printing expands them into sustained, exhibition-ready works. In parallel, the project can include time-lapse videos of patina progression and small magnetic plate sculptures that extend the same language into object and installation form.

The work is also concerned with semiotics. The stamped motifs are not presented as literal symbols with fixed meanings; rather, they operate as partial signs, visually suggestive structures that prompt viewers to assemble their own interpretations. This makes the work less about illustration than about the viewer’s movement between recognition and uncertainty. The plate is therefore not only a support for image-making, but a site where time, signification, and material instability remain visibly active.

## Strategic Core

The practice should now be presented as a multi-part body with one primary spine and two secondary extensions:
- Primary: steel plates and archival prints.
- Secondary: time-lapse documentation of patina progression.
- Secondary: small magnetic constructions / plate-based sculptures.

The time dimension is not an add-on. It is one of the work’s central propositions, because the plate keeps changing while the viewer encounters it. The semiotic dimension of the stamped motifs should also be named more clearly: the marks function as fragments of a sign system, inviting viewers to participate in making meaning.

## Updated Direction

- Keep the 4 x 12 plate format as the launch standard for now.
- Use prints as the main exhibition format.
- Use plates as source objects and, where strong enough, as exhibited works.
- Add time-lapse video selectively where it clarifies the temporal logic.
- Develop magnetic plate constructions as an experimental satellite form.
