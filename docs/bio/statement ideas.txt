
Artist Statement

Current:
"I work with steel plates as living surfaces. By brushing on copper sulfate, I coax patinas to bloom and creep across the surface. Onto this shifting ground I stamp geometric patterns that I find or fabricate. In this process, controlled marks meet unpredictable natural processes, creating vibrant areas of interference. The metal responds with subtle shifts of color, earthy reds, greens, and blues that emerge from the oxidation itself, creating ethereal scenes and landscapes that are revealed rather than made. The recurring motifs I press into the surface look like the remnants of a lost language, engaging the viewer in a hunt for meaning.",
"What drives me is the dialogue between human intervention and natural forces, the inherent tension transforming its component parts into something entirely new. The process is inspired by Hegel's theory of synthesis, with my works developing organically rather than being built up, each piece capturing a moment of becoming, where metal, chemistry, and time converge into something neither fully controlled nor entirely accidental.",
"Through the mystery of the unfinished and the uncertain, I aim to reawaken a sense of wonder in what is transient and ephemeral. Capturing fleeting states of material becoming, I invite contemplation of memory, surface, and time, and the enduring beauty found in uncertainty."


Version 3: 
I work with chemically induced patinas on metal plates, composing through the tension between controlled intervention and unpredictable natural processes. By impressing patterns and masks onto the evolving surfaces, I generate fields of interference where ordered, deterministic forms meet the stochastic, amorphous growth of oxidation. Subtle shifts of color—earthy reds, ochres, blues, and greens—arise organically from the metal itself, producing landscapes that feel at once ancient and newly discovered.
My practice is rooted in a fascination with the interplay between human intention and natural entropy. Fragments of lattice-like structures and symbols resembling an undeciphered language emerge within the patina, inviting viewers to search for meaning in a space where clarity remains just out of reach. This unresolved dialogue between control and chaos reflects a larger inquiry into how systems of order coexist with forces of decay, echoing philosophical frameworks such as Hegel’s synthesis of opposing forces.
Through this process, I aim to reawaken a sense of wonder in what is unfinished, fragile, or in flux. The works are not built through accumulation but through transformation, recording ephemeral states of material becoming. In them, I invite contemplation of memory, surface, and time, and the enduring beauty found in uncertainty.


Version 2:
I am a contemporary artist composing on metal plates by impressing patterns onto chemically-induced patinas. The interplay of the static, deterministic stamp pattern with the dynamic, stochastic growth of the patina creates vibrant, amorphous areas of interference. The entire surface is subtly colored by the oxidation of the metal substrate. It is within these evolving, coded landscapes that my work unfolds, inviting contemplation on the raw, visceral beauty found in the balance between intention and unpredictability.
My fascination with the natural world's patterns and their interaction with projections of my own imagination has always driven my artistic exploration. To engage the viewer in this profound dialogue, I weave my patterns with the symbols of unknown languages, to evoke the subconscious into participating in the hunt for meaning. Through this interplay of control and chaos—a reflection of Hegelian synthesis—my work seeks to evoke an ancient mystery, to rekindle a forgotten sense of innocent awe and wonder. It is a process of creation not through accumulation, but of development through transformation and synthesis.

The resulting works resemble fragments of forgotten languages or landscapes in flux, inviting viewers to reflect on beauty, memory, and transformation.

Expanded version 1:
My practice investigates the dialogue between intentional intervention and uncontrolled natural transformation. Using ferrous metals as a substrate, I impress patterned structures into chemically induced patinas, orchestrating moments where order collides with entropy. These surfaces—subtly colored by oxidation—become sites of emergence, where amorphous forms and cryptic marks appear, only to dissolve again into flux.
The work draws upon philosophical notions of synthesis, particularly the Hegelian interplay of thesis and antithesis, to reflect on how meaning arises from tension rather than resolution. Viewers are invited to inhabit this space of ambiguity, where fragments of imagined languages, half-formed symbols, and organic flows suggest narratives that resist closure.
Ultimately, the works are meditations on material becoming. They are not constructed by accumulation but revealed through transformation—moments of balance between fragility and permanence, memory and erasure, control and chance.

Expanded version 2:
I work with metal plates, allowing chemical patinas to develop across surfaces where I have impressed patterns and forms. In this process, controlled marks meet unpredictable oxidation—my stamps and masks guiding the chemistry while natural forces take over. The metal responds with subtle shifts of color: earthy reds, greens, and blues that emerge from the oxidation itself, creating what feel like landscapes discovered rather than made.
What draws me is the dialogue between intention and chance. The lattice patterns I press into the surface become part of something larger as the patina grows around them—like fragments of a language being written and rewritten by time itself. There's a philosophical echo here of Hegel's synthesis, where opposing forces generate something new, but I'm most interested in the visual poetry that emerges from this collaboration with materials.
These works aren't built up but revealed through transformation. Each piece captures a moment of becoming—when metal, chemistry, and time converge into something neither fully controlled nor entirely accidental. I invite viewers into this space between order and mystery, where beauty lives in the unfinished and the uncertain.



Additional info:

Materials - Which metals? Currently only mild (carbon) steel, but also experimenting with and planning to expand into copper and brass. Which chemicals? Currently copper sulfate (sulphate), muriatic acid and phosphoric acid,  but also experimenting with ammonia, liver of sulphur, and many other patina-inducing chemicals. Which tools? The method involves either immersing the metal in the chemicals or brushing the chemicals onto the surface. The stamps are pattern drawings extruded and printed with a 3D printer. The typical size of the metal plates is 4-8 inches wide by 12-16 inches high. The current series names are Strange Attractors, Across the Garden Wall, Cosmic Debris, and Beyond the Reef.
Visual references - The stamp patterns are repetitive geometric motifs that are recognizably man-made. The intent is for the viewer to perceive in them symbols that look like a language, or at least some form of communication. However, this is just a tease, a ruse to engage the viewer's subconscious in creating the narrative for the composition. Using semiotic terminology, the patterns are composed signifiers that refer to unresolved, unknown signifieds.
