

Current: 

I work with chemically induced patinas on metal plates, composing through the tension between controlled intervention and unpredictable natural processes. By impressing patterns and masks onto the evolving surfaces, I generate fields of interference where ordered, deterministic forms meet the stochastic, amorphous growth of oxidation. Subtle shifts of color—earthy reds, ochres, blues, and greens—arise organically from the metal itself, producing landscapes that feel at once ancient and newly discovered.
My practice is rooted in a fascination with the interplay between human intention and natural entropy. Fragments of lattice-like structures and symbols resembling an undeciphered language emerge within the patina, inviting viewers to search for meaning in a space where clarity remains just out of reach. This unresolved dialogue between control and chaos reflects a larger inquiry into how systems of order coexist with forces of decay, echoing philosophical frameworks such as Hegel’s synthesis of opposing forces.
Through this process, I aim to reawaken a sense of wonder in what is unfinished, fragile, or in flux. The works are not built through accumulation but through transformation, recording ephemeral states of material becoming. In them, I invite contemplation of memory, surface, and time, and the enduring beauty found in uncertainty.


Version 2

I am a contemporary artist composing on metal plates by impressing patterns onto chemically-induced patinas. The interplay of the static, deterministic stamp pattern with the dynamic, stochastic growth of the patina creates vibrant, amorphous areas of interference. The entire surface is subtly colored by the oxidation of the metal substrate. It is within these evolving, coded landscapes that my work unfolds, inviting contemplation on the raw, visceral beauty found in the balance between intention and unpredictability.
My fascination with the natural world's patterns and their interaction with projections of my own imagination has always driven my artistic exploration. To engage the viewer in this profound dialogue, I weave my patterns with the symbols of unknown languages, to evoke the subconscious into participating in the hunt for meaning. Through this interplay of control and chaos—a reflection of Hegelian synthesis—my work seeks to evoke an ancient mystery, to rekindle a forgotten sense of innocent awe and wonder. It is a process of creation not through accumulation, but of development through transformation and synthesis.
