BIO

Andrei Pancu (b. 1968, Bucharest, Romania) is a contemporary artist based in Ottawa, Canada, who induces patination reactions on metal plates to present a unified inquiry into the raw, visceral nature of surface, memory, and perception. His process is not one of creation through accumulation, but of development through synthesis, pitting the stochastic flow of the patina against the deterministic application of stamps and masks. Pancu's works are dynamic fields of textures and flows in a constant state of flux. Forms emerge from and dissolve into this rich, fertile ground, like echoes of events suspended in time. Fragments of lattice-like structures form a cryptic language of ambiguous signifiers, teasing the viewer into resolving unresolvable references. The muted, earthy palette of rusted reds, deep ochres, and muted blues arises organically from the oxidation of the metal substrate, weaving a narrative of resilience and ruin. For Pancu, the surface is the plate in the darkroom where the image develops. He photographs the progression of the patina throughout the process, capturing ephemeral states from which he creates limited-edition prints.

The interaction of a free-flowing, disorderly system with an ordered, opposing force has always been both the subject and process of Andrei Pancu's work. Embracing the struggle to counter the flow of entropy in everyday life, he explores the desperate impulse to establish and maintain a semblance of order and stability. For his seminal series Strange Attractors (2024) and Across the Garden Wall (2025) Pancu embodies Hegel's concept of synthesis from thesis and antithesis, creating tension between the entropy of the patina and the order imposed by the artist's will to elevate the inherent truths found in both, and integrate them into a comprehensive, higher level of understanding. Refusing to provide a clean, easy visual narrative, Pancu requires the viewer to lean in, to confront the uncomfortable beauty in what is broken and unfinished.

Andrei Pancu studied Fine Arts at the Université de Montréal.

Additional info:

Materials - Which metals? Currently only mild (carbon) steel, but also experimenting with and planning to expand into copper and brass. Which chemicals? Currently copper sulfate (sulphate), muriatic acid and phosphoric acid,  but also experimenting with ammonia, liver of sulphur, and many other patina-inducing chemicals. Which tools? The method involves either immersing the metal in the chemicals or brushing the chemicals onto the surface. The stamps are pattern drawings extruded and printed with a 3D printer. The typical size of the metal plates is 4-8 inches wide by 12-16 inches high. The current series names are Strange Attractors, Across the Garden Wall, Cosmic Debris, and Beyond the Reef.
Visual references - The stamp patterns are repetitive geometric motifs that are recognizably man-made. The intent is for the viewer to perceive in them symbols that look like a language, or at least some form of communication. However, this is just a tease, a ruse to engage the viewer's subconscious in creating the narrative for the composition. Using semiotic terminology, the patterns are composed signifiers that refer to unresolved, unknown signifieds.
